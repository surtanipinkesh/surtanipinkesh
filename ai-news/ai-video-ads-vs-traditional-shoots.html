AI video ads vs traditional shoots: what really changes

AI video has moved from a novelty to a real option for brand ads. Here is a plain comparison with a traditional shoot: what gets faster and cheaper, what you can now test, and where a camera crew is still the better choice.

By Papad Pixels · 3 min read

In 30 seconds

  • A traditional shoot needs crew, talent, locations and equipment booked for set days; an AI video ad is made from references, prompts and editing.
  • AI production usually takes days, not weeks, and changes are made in the edit instead of on a reshoot.
  • Because each extra version is cheap, brands can test several hooks, styles and formats instead of betting on one cut.
  • AI still struggles with specific real people, exact real locations and very long continuous shots, so a shoot is better for those.
  • Many brands now mix the two: film the hero moments and use AI for variations, cutdowns and new scenes.

How each one is made

A traditional shoot starts with pre-production: a script, a location, casting, a crew and kit booked for specific days. Everything happens on set, and what you capture is what you have to work with in the edit. Changing a scene later usually means booking another day.

An AI video ad starts the same way, with an idea and a script, but the footage is generated instead of filmed. Products and characters are kept consistent with reference images, each shot is created and refined, and then it is edited, graded and mixed like any other commercial. The edit is where most of the craft now sits.

The key differences

  • Speed: no booking, travel or set days. A short social ad can be ready in a few days, and a polished commercial in about a week.
  • Cost: there is no crew, location, talent or equipment bill, so the budget goes to ideas, editing and sound. As a reference, our social videos start from $295 and AI commercials from $595 (see pricing).
  • Changes: a new background, product colour or ending is an edit, not a reshoot.
  • Testing: you can make several hooks, styles or lengths of the same idea and let real ad results pick the winner.
  • Formats: 9:16 for Reels, TikTok and Shorts and 16:9 for YouTube and TV come from the same project.
  • Worlds you cannot film: product close-ups that would need special rigs, impossible locations, or stylised looks are as easy as a studio shot.

Where a traditional shoot still wins

AI is not the right tool for everything. If the ad must show a specific real person, such as a founder, an athlete or a customer, or an exact real place like your own store, filming is still the honest and reliable option.

Very long continuous takes, live events and documentary-style stories also belong to a camera. And for tightly regulated categories, brands should check what their platforms and markets allow for AI-generated content and label it where needed.

How brands are combining both

The most effective approach we see is a mix. Film the moments that need to be real, then use AI for the rest: new scenes, seasonal versions, product variants, cutdowns and test hooks. One shoot day can turn into months of fresh ads.

Questions people ask

Is an AI video ad cheaper than a traditional shoot?

Usually yes, because there is no crew, location, talent or equipment to pay for. The cost depends mainly on length, number of scenes and the deliverables you need.

How long does it take to make an AI video ad?

A short social ad can be ready in a few days and a polished 30 to 60 second commercial in about a week, with rush options for urgent launches.

Can AI video show my real product?

Yes. Your product photos are used as references so its shape, colour and label stay consistent in every shot.

When should I still film a traditional shoot?

When the ad must show a specific real person or real place, for live events, and for long documentary-style stories.
